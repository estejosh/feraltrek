LEGO Grant Proposal: eth-hw-signer

A $40,000 grant request to build open firmware and a host service that keep Ethereum validator keys on dedicated microcontroller boards.

This LEGO grant proposal, responding to Lido RFP 12209, asks for $40,000 to build eth-hw-signer: open-source firmware on STM32U5 boards that hold validator keys, plus a Rust host service speaking the Web3Signer-compatible remote-signing API (v1.4+), so validator clients need no changes. According to the proposal, keys are imported once, sealed inside the device and never exported; the device derives the signing root itself and keeps its own forward-only slashing-protection watermark. The team commits to at least 100 validators on physical boards running 30 days of live duties on Hoodi without a slashable signature, with a target of about 500 validators plus standby on 10 boards, dependent on a signing benchmark on the real chip. It would ask Lido NOM for Hoodi test stake. The 30-day mark falls about 3 to 3.5 months after approval, with maintenance promised for 2 years. The RFP was written after two node operators exited Lido validators following infrastructure compromises.

Flags: grant spend 40,000 USD, performance target depends on unbenchmarked hardware
